This case study documents enforcement activity at 308 4th Ave S, Seattle — a high-rise residential property in Council District 7 managed under GRE Downtowners LLC — from the first recorded complaints through the period following a January 2020 fire.

STLCA reviewed complaint records from SDCI's publicly available Complaints & Violations Built Environment dataset, SFD incident data, and records from the Seattle Services Portal. The timeline of documented events raises questions about enforcement sequencing, response intervals, and the completeness of the public record following the fire.

All findings in this report are drawn from public records. This report does not allege intent. It documents what the record shows and what it does not show.

Section 01 — Property Record

308 4th Ave S — The Public Record

308 4th Ave S is a multi-story residential building located in Seattle's District 7. The property appears in SDCI's enforcement database under the ownership of GRE Downtowners LLC. The first complaints in the publicly available record date to 2016, shortly after Seattle's landmark Ordinance 125054 took effect.

Over the following three years, the building accumulated more than 136 complaint records covering elevator failures, pest infestations, heat outages, trash accumulation, and related habitability conditions. Many of those complaints did not result in a documented inspection.

Concurrently, Seattle Fire Department incident logs document 133 elevator rescues at the address between 2016 and 2022. SFD responds to elevator rescues as emergency calls. The volume and sustained duration of those events — averaging roughly 20 per year over six years — represents a notable concentration at a single residential address.

Residents with mobility limitations were disproportionately affected. When elevators failed, wheelchair users and others with physical disabilities were unable to exit their floors independently. Some were trapped for hours awaiting SFD response. Each of those 133 rescue calls represents a tenant held in their building by conditions SDCI was notified about and did not systematically address.

The fire safety failures also exposed first responders to documented risk. Seattle firefighters repeatedly entered a building with known blocked exit pathways and compromised egress routes — conditions SFD had formally reported to SDCI in November 2019. When SDCI does not enforce corrections, the hazards it documents do not disappear; they accumulate, and emergency personnel who respond to future incidents bear the physical consequence.

Post-Fire Record
In the period following the January 7, 2020 fire, complaints that had appeared in the SDCI database began disappearing from public records. Inspector "Steven" closed cases without documented resolution or follow-up inspection records. The systematic erasure of complaint records after a fire — at a property with a documented multi-year enforcement history — raises questions the available public record does not answer. No financial penalties, civil fines, or formal enforcement orders against GRE Downtowners LLC appear in the public record for the pre-fire or post-fire period.
The 5-Step Pattern: (1) Complaints filed. (2) Agency delays response. (3) Violations continue without consequence. (4) Crisis occurs. (5) Records vanish. The public record at 308 4th Ave S follows this pattern precisely.
2024
Owner Legal Action

No documentation of financial penalties against the owner appears in the public SDCI record as of the filing date.

The Notice of Violation was issued December 13, 2019. The compliance deadline was January 13, 2020. The fire occurred January 7 — six days before the owner was required to correct the violations.

Section 03 — Record Analysis

What the Record Documents

The following findings are drawn directly from the documentary record. Each finding identifies what the record shows and, where relevant, what the record does not show.

1

29-Day Response Interval Following SFD Life-Safety Alert

SFD notified SDCI of fire safety concerns — specifically blocked egress — on November 6, 2019. The first documented SDCI inspection occurred December 5, 2019. The 29-day interval between a fire department notification of blocked exits at a residential building and a code compliance inspection is documented in the public record. SDCI does not publish enforceable response time standards for life-safety complaints from other City departments in a form that permits direct assessment of compliance.

2

NOV Compliance Timeline Placed Deadline After Fire Date

The December 13, 2019 Notice of Violation set a compliance deadline of January 13, 2020. The fire occurred January 7, 2020. The enforcement structure, as issued, required the owner to remediate identified violations by a date six days after the building burned. The record does not reflect any emergency order, interim protective action, or accelerated compliance requirement issued between the NOV date and the fire date.

3

69% of Complaints Closed Without Documented Inspection

Across all complaint records at 308 4th Ave S, approximately 69% of closed complaints do not include a documented inspection date or inspection result in the public SDCI dataset. This is consistent with citywide patterns in STLCA's analysis — the citywide uninspected closure rate for landlord-tenant complaints is 59.8%. The Addison on 4th rate exceeds the citywide baseline by approximately 9 percentage points.

4

133 SFD Elevator Rescues Without Documented SDCI Enforcement Action

Seattle Fire Department records document 133 elevator rescue calls at 308 4th Ave S between 2016 and 2022. SFD elevator rescue dispatch is a life-safety response. The SDCI public record does not contain a documented enforcement action specifically arising from the sustained pattern of elevator failures across this period. The first documented SDCI enforcement action citing fire/egress conditions was the December 2019 NOV — issued following SFD's November 2019 notification, after six years of elevator incidents.

5

Post-Fire Complaints Closed Without Documented Follow-Up

Following the January 7, 2020 fire, complaint records associated with the property were closed in the SDCI database. The public record for those closures does not include documented inspection results. The record does not contain evidence that SDCI requested, received, or published a post-fire enforcement summary or accountability review for this property.

6

Zero Financial Penalties in Public Record

The SDCI public complaint database does not document any civil penalty, financial fine, or monetary enforcement action against GRE Downtowners LLC across the full period of record from 2016 through 2025. This is notable given the complaint volume, SFD rescue frequency, Notice of Violation, and fire at the property. Seattle Municipal Code authorizes civil penalties of up to $150 per day per violation for housing code non-compliance.

STLCA's analysis of the post-fire period found that complaint records associated with 308 4th Ave S were closed in the SDCI database without populated inspection result fields. This pattern is consistent with citywide administrative closure practices, but is notable given the severity and public nature of the January 7, 2020 fire event.

Section 05 — Subsequent Legal Proceedings

Owner Litigation — 2024

In 2024, GRE Downtowners LLC filed a legal action against the City of Seattle. The suit alleged that the City's affordable housing regulations had imposed economic hardship on the property owner.

At the time the suit was filed, the public record for the property included:

  • 136+ complaints filed with SDCI
  • 133 SFD elevator rescue incidents
  • At least one Notice of Violation (December 2019)
  • A building fire (January 7, 2020)
  • Zero documented financial penalties against the owner

This report does not assess the legal merits of the lawsuit. The litigation is documented here because it is part of the public record and because the juxtaposition of the enforcement history with the legal claim — that housing regulations caused hardship — is relevant context for the City Council's assessment of how SDCI's enforcement record interacted with this property over the period in question.

Section 06 — Systemic Context

How This Property Fits the Citywide Pattern

The Addison on 4th is not an isolated case. STLCA's citywide analysis of 233,543 SDCI complaint records spanning 2003–2025 documents a consistent pattern: the majority of landlord-tenant complaints close without a documented inspection, regardless of complaint severity, complaint volume, or prior NOV history at the address.

The citywide uninspected closure rate for landlord-tenant complaints is 59.8%. At 308 4th Ave S, the rate is approximately 69% — nearly 10 points above the already-elevated citywide baseline. The property also demonstrates a pattern seen across STLCA's high-complaint-volume address sample: elevated complaint counts do not reliably trigger escalated enforcement response.

What This Record Raises

The documentary record at 308 4th Ave S presents several questions that the available public records do not answer. Why did 29 days elapse between a fire department notification of blocked egress and the first SDCI inspection? Why was the compliance deadline set six days after the date of the fire? Why did 133 SFD elevator rescues not generate a documented SDCI enforcement action specifically addressing the elevator condition?

This report does not claim that SDCI personnel acted improperly. It documents what the record shows and what the record does not show. The absence of documentation is itself a data point — one that the annual Director's Reports required under Ordinance 125054 were designed to surface. Those reports were never filed.
