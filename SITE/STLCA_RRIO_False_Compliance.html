RRIO Program Analysis - March 2026

How RRIO Produces
False Compliance

The Rental Registration and Inspection Ordinance is publicly described as proactive enforcement. The public record documents a different function: a certification mechanism that signals compliance to City Council while conditions on the ground remain unresolved. This is not a series of isolated failures. It is the system operating as structured.

Enforcement Documentation Collapse
Inspection Documentation Rate - LLT Complaints 2003-2025
Peak: 90.1% of LLT complaints had documented inspection results in 2013. By 2018 the rate collapsed to 24.8%. By 2025: 15.1%. Complaint volume rose 361% over the same period. The StatusCurrent field - the administrative label - remained 100% complete every year. The evidence of enforcement did not.

Six Documented Structural Failures

Finding 1
Certification Divorced from Habitability
RRIO certificates are issued to properties with active Notices of Violation and unresolved complaints. Compliance status is determined by landlord self-reporting. No independent unit-level inspection is required prior to certification. The certificate signals compliance to Council and the public; the complaint record documents conditions that never changed.
Finding 2
Inspection Data Structurally Incomplete
Critical data fields - last inspection date, inspection type, inspection outcome - are missing, blank, or inconsistently populated. 62.3% of all LLT cases have no inspection date recorded. Without these fields, performance reporting is impossible and Council oversight operates without reliable information.
Finding 3
Complaints Closed Without Verification
High-priority habitability complaints - loss of hot water, flooding, fire hazards - are documented as closed "withdrawn," "no contact," or "no violation found" while conditions persisted. Inspectors accepted landlord representations without independent verification. Tenant confirmation was not obtained before closure.
Finding 4
Timelines Unenforced, Fines Uncollected
Notices of Violation routinely exceed statutory compliance deadlines without fine escalation or referral to stronger enforcement tools. Millions of dollars in assessed penalties remain uncollected. The result is a documented incentive structure where delay carries no cost and noncompliance is functionally risk-free.
Finding 5
Reported Performance Misleads
SDCI reporting emphasizes "properties inspected" rather than "units inspected." In large multifamily buildings, only a fraction of units are examined. When measured at the unit level, inspection coverage drops to a negligible percentage of the rental housing stock - undermining public and Council confidence in reported outcomes.
Finding 6
Private Inspector Conflict of Interest
RRIO allows landlords to hire private inspectors they pay directly. 81,872 RRIO records involve private inspector use. The RRIO checklist is also documented as narrower in scope than the full Housing Maintenance and Building Code - meaning a property can pass RRIO while failing the broader habitability standard tenants are legally entitled to.
The system produces the outcome it was structured to produce.
StatusCurrent - the administrative label - is 100% complete in SDCI records every year, all years. It always says "Completed" or "Closed." The inspection fields that would document whether anyone actually looked at anything are blank in 62% of cases. The label functions. The enforcement does not. This distinction is the entire case.
