An Independent Analysis of 20 Years of SDCI Enforcement Records

STLCA analyzed 233,543 SDCI complaint records spanning 2003-2025. We are an independent, non-partisan data oversight initiative. We are here as a resource and a partner. Every number in this briefing is derived from SDCI's own public data and is independently replicable from the Seattle Open Data Portal.

Who We Are

STLCA analyzes publicly available City of Seattle enforcement records to produce structured, verifiable assessments of how code compliance is documented, and where the public record cannot confirm that enforcement occurred. We are not a tenant advocacy organization. We do not represent any party in any proceeding. We present what the data shows, measured against itself and against the legal obligations of the agencies responsible for maintaining it.

We are here as a partner and a resource. The patterns documented in this briefing developed across multiple administrations. We are not assigning blame to this Council or to any individual. We are delivering verified evidence and offering to work with you on next steps. No independent body has previously delivered this level of structured analysis from SDCI's own public data. We have built something that can be used immediately.

What We Found

What We Built

We analyzed 233,543 SDCI enforcement records and produced a structured accountability analysis. In doing so, we identified that Seattle Municipal Code Section 22.206.180 required SDCI to deliver exactly this kind of report to this Council every year beginning June 15, 2017.

That report was never filed. Not once in nine years.

What follows is that report - built from SDCI's own data.

Ordinance 125054 - Section 9 - Required Annual Report Elements
What the law required SDCI to report to this Council every year beginning June 15, 2017 -- and what STLCA found from the public record
Note on Section 9: Section 9 was not part of the original bill. It was added as a standalone amendment authored specifically by a District 1 council member at the time. It was written as its own section with the sole purpose of creating a mandatory annual accountability mechanism. The reporting deadline was negotiated during the amendment process and set at June 15 of each year, beginning 2017. The Council passed the full ordinance 8-0. Section 9 has never been fulfilled.
Required by Ordinance Status What STLCA Found From Public Record
Number of inquiries from tenants and landlords Available 41,653 LLT complaints filed (2003-2025). 58,761 total LLT records including NOVs, citations, and relocation orders. Sourced from SDCI open data, 233,543 total records.
Number of inspections conducted related to those inquiries Available 62.3% of LLT records (36,633 of 58,761) have no inspection date and no documented result -- no evidence in the public record that enforcement produced any outcome. An additional 9 records have an inspection date but no result logged. 22,119 records (37.6%) have a documented inspection result. Peak documentation rate: 90.1% in 2013. Rate as of 2025: 15.1%.
Outcomes of those inspections Available 59.8% of closed LLT cases have no documented inspection result -- the case is "Completed" or "Closed" in the record with no evidence of what occurred. 11,341 Notices of Violation issued citywide. 48 cases ever formally referred to law in 20+ years. 5,936 LLT cases remain open.
Housing cost increases delayed based on Department determination Not in data Not in the public record -- and that is the finding. Section 9 required SDCI to track and report housing cost increases delayed under 22.206.180.J. The absence of this data does not mean STLCA could not find it. It means SDCI never built the tracking system the ordinance required. There is no record to find.
Refunds or credits ordered from landlords Not in data Not in the public record -- and that is the finding. Required annually. No tracking system was created. No refund or credit data has ever been compiled or disclosed to the Council.
Eviction cross-reference -- did SDCI receive complaints at addresses where evictions occurred Not in data Not in the public record -- and that is the finding. Section 9 specifically required SDCI to obtain eviction records, cross-reference them against complaint addresses, and determine whether the eviction resulted from a 22.206.180.J failure. This cross-reference was never performed. No methodology was ever built. SDCI has no public record of ever attempting it.
Budget allocations for the program Not in data Not published in open data. Not included in any report to Council.
What This Table Shows

Of the seven required annual report elements, STLCA can document three from SDCI's own public records. The remaining four -- housing cost delays, refunds ordered, eviction cross-referencing, and budget allocations -- do not exist in the public record. This is not a gap in STLCA's research. It is evidence that SDCI never built the tracking and reporting infrastructure that Section 9 required. The council member who authored that section wrote it specifically to create accountability. The accountability mechanism was never implemented. "Completed" and "Closed" status labels appear in 100% of records every year. Documented inspection results do not. The label is always there. The evidence is not.

The 2018 Enforcement Collapse

The data does not show a gradual drift in enforcement quality. It shows a structural break. Using SDCI’s own records, a clear before-and-after emerges at 2018.

Ordinance 125054 — The Missing Reports

SMC 22.206.180 — Required Annual Reporting to Council
Ordinance 125054 • Passed 8-0, June 2016 • Effective July 10, 2016 • First report due: June 15, 2017
→
The ordinance required the SDCI Director to report annually to City Council. Required elements: inquiry counts, inspections conducted, outcomes, rent increases delayed, refunds ordered, eviction cross-referencing, and budget allocations.
→
Zero reports were filed. Not one, in nine years. From 2017 through 2025, SDCI did not produce a single annual report to Council as required by this ordinance.
→
When contacted, SDCI’s RRIO Manager responded in writing: “We’ve never prepared such a report… 22.206.180.J doesn’t exist… makes a jump from I to K.” The signed ordinance is a public record and is available in this repository.
→
The data to produce these reports existed and was publicly available throughout this period. The reports were not missing because the data did not exist. The reports were not produced.

Financial Context

Seattle is operating under budget constraints that have required reductions across city services. SDCI’s enforcement function — including the collection of fines, penalties, and citation revenue from documented violations — depends at every stage on whether violations are documented, pursued to resolution, and followed through to collection.

When 59.8% of closed landlord-tenant cases carry no documented inspection result, the enforcement chain that leads to fine issuance, collection, and compliance is broken at its earliest step. The absence of documentation is not a neutral outcome. It forecloses everything downstream.

STLCA does not assert a specific uncollected revenue figure citywide. What the public record shows is that the documentation infrastructure required to pursue enforcement outcomes was absent in the majority of closed LLT cases across the entire 20-year record. The Council is positioned to direct SDCI to quantify what that has cost.

STLCA’s Role

No auditor has previously delivered this level of analysis to the Council from SDCI’s own public data. STLCA has normalized, classified, and structured 20+ years of complaint records, cross-referenced against RRIO registration data, organized by council district, and documented against the legal obligations imposed by ordinances passed by prior City Councils that remain standing law.

That work is now public, permanent, and available to every council member, every Seattle resident, every journalist, and every policymaker who wants to understand what the SDCI enforcement record actually shows. We are here to continue that work in partnership with this Council.

What We Are Asking For

What We Are Asking — 30-Day Response Window
1
Enforce the standing legal obligation under Ordinance 125054. The 2016 City Council required annual SDCI reporting under SMC 22.206.180 with an 8-0 vote. That law is still on the books. We are asking this Council to direct SDCI to produce those reports — or provide a formal written explanation of why the obligation has not been met for nine consecutive years. You did not pass this ordinance. You inherited it. The obligation runs to the Council as an institution, and it has never been fulfilled.
2
Request a briefing. STLCA is prepared to present to the full Council, individual district offices, or the relevant oversight committee. We will answer any question about our methodology, our data, or our findings. All of it is on the table.
3
Direct SDCI to explain its documentation standards. Specifically: what constitutes a documented enforcement action under SDCI’s own policies, and what accounts for the structural break in inspection documentation beginning in 2018?
4
Consider what independent oversight of SDCI would look like. Seattle has an Office of Inspector General for SPD. The question of whether an agency that issues permits and enforces habitability for the same properties can self-report its own performance is a standard governance concern. STLCA is prepared to serve as a data partner and resource as the Council considers what independent oversight of SDCI should look like — and we can continue this work at no cost to the City.
