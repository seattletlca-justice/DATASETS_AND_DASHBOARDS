Section 01 — The Evictions

What Happened at Everspring Inn

Everspring Inn was home to a population of tenants who depended on the property for stable, long-term housing. During the COVID-19 pandemic, Seattle — along with the State of Washington — enacted emergency moratoriums and protections specifically to prevent exactly the kind of displacement that occurred at this property.

STLCA's review of the available record documents a mass eviction pattern that proceeded despite these protections. The regulatory mechanism that should have intervened — SDCI's enforcement apparatus — failed to do so in a manner consistent with the protections tenants were legally entitled to.

  • Unlawful mass evictions: Tenants were evicted during the COVID-19 pandemic in apparent violation of emergency protections in force at the time.
  • Life safety neglect: Concurrent life safety complaints at the property were not addressed in a manner consistent with standard enforcement expectations.
  • Data manipulation: STLCA identified evidence that records associated with the Everspring evictions and complaints were manipulated — a finding documented in STLCA's compiled case files.
  • Regulatory abandonment: The regulatory response to conditions at Everspring represented a systemic failure to use available enforcement tools to protect residents.

Section 02 — Context

Part of a Citywide Pattern

The Everspring case did not occur in isolation. STLCA's citywide analysis found that eviction-related complaints across all seven Council districts are closed without documented inspections at rates exceeding 85%. The Everspring Inn is one of several properties where this systemic failure had direct human consequences for tenants who had no other protective recourse.

The same enforcement gaps that enabled the Addison fire and the Missing Reports pattern also enabled the Everspring evictions. STLCA's full case report will document the complete connection between these patterns and the specific facts of this case.
