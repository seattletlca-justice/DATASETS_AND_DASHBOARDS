This case study documents SDCI enforcement activity at 8201 Aurora Ave N during August 2020 — a period when Washington State's COVID-19 emergency eviction moratorium was in effect. The moratorium prohibited landlords from evicting tenants in most circumstances.

STLCA reviewed 13 complaint records filed on August 18, 2020 and subsequently available through the Seattle Services Portal and SDCI's public complaints dataset. The records document a range of conditions: hot water shutoff, armed security personnel, boarded windows, locked-out tenants, and allegations of mass displacement. All 13 complaints were closed within 14 days. The public record does not contain documentation of a field inspection for any of the 13 complaints.

All findings in this report are drawn from public records. All complaint descriptions cited reflect the language of the original complaint records. This report does not allege intent. It documents what the record shows and what it does not show.

Section 01 — Legal and Regulatory Context

The COVID-19 Eviction Moratorium

Applicable Law — August 2020

Washington State Governor Jay Inslee issued Proclamation 20-19 on March 18, 2020, prohibiting residential evictions statewide as part of the COVID-19 emergency response. The moratorium was extended and amended through subsequent proclamations. As of August 2020, the moratorium remained in effect and prohibited landlords from issuing eviction notices, initiating eviction proceedings, or taking actions that would effectively displace residential tenants in most circumstances.

The City of Seattle had also enacted additional local protections. Seattle Municipal Code tenant-landlord provisions prohibited self-help eviction — defined as any action by a landlord to remove a tenant without a court order, including lockouts, utility shutoffs, and removal of tenant belongings. The SMC does not require a formal eviction notice to have occurred; a tenant displaced through any of these means retains the right to lodge a complaint with SDCI.

8201 Aurora Ave N was operating as a residential motel — a property type in which tenants residing 30 or more days acquire legal tenancy rights under Washington State law, regardless of how the accommodation is marketed. Multiple tenants at the Everspring Inn had established legal tenancy.

The property had a complaint history at SDCI dating to at least 2013 — seven years before the August 2020 events. The owner of record, Myung Ha Kang (also known as Ryan Kang), had operated the property through the period of that prior complaint history without documented enforcement action escalating to formal sanctions.

Complaint records from August 2020 describe tenants who had resided at the property for months or years. Several complaints reference tenants with Washington State identification cards showing the Everspring Inn address — documentation of established residency. At least one complainant identifies as a disabled senior resident.

The August 2020 complaints allege that the owner told tenants they had 24 hours to vacate, that hot water was shut off building-wide on August 8, that the owner falsely claimed City of Seattle was ordering the building closed, and that armed security accompanied the owner in the halls during the displacement period.

Section 04 — Record Analysis

What the Record Documents

The following findings are drawn directly from the documentary record. Each finding identifies what the record shows and, where relevant, what the record does not show.

1

13 Complaints Closed in 14 Days — Zero Inspections Documented

Thirteen complaints were filed on August 18, 2020, describing conditions consistent with unlawful self-help eviction during an active state eviction moratorium. All 13 were closed within 14 days — the first on August 19 (one day after filing); the remaining 12 on September 1. The public SDCI record does not contain a documented inspection date or inspection result for any of the 13 complaints. The SDCI complaints dataset fields LastInspDate and LastInspResult are blank for all 13 records.

2

"Voluntary Compliance" Applied to Cases of Physical Displacement

The closure designation "Admin Closure / Voluntary Compliance" was applied to 12 of the 13 complaints by Inspector Stella Washington on September 1, 2020. The closure designation "Voluntary Compliance" typically indicates that the party responsible for the violation has corrected the condition. The complaint descriptions for these records document tenants who had already been displaced from the property — in some cases with their rooms boarded and windows sealed. The record does not explain how "Voluntary Compliance" was established in the absence of a documented inspection.

3

One Complaint Closed the Day After Filing — Complainant Had Provided Residency Documentation

Record 007177-20CP was filed August 18, 2020 by a complainant identifying as a disabled senior resident of Room 305. The complaint notes that the complainant had provided receipts and a Washington State identification card showing the Everspring Inn as their address — establishing legal tenancy. The complaint was closed by James Hammon on August 19, 2020 — one day after filing — as "Admin Closure." The public record contains no inspection documentation for this record.

4

Events Occurred During Active State Eviction Moratorium

Washington State Proclamation 20-19 and its amendments prohibited residential evictions throughout August 2020. The complaint descriptions document conditions that — if accurate — would constitute violations of the moratorium: posting eviction notices, shutting off utilities, using security personnel to compel departure, and boarding occupied or recently occupied units. The public record does not document that SDCI communicated the existence of the moratorium to the owner or investigated potential moratorium violations as part of the complaint response.

5

July 2021 NOV Documents 148 Violations — 11 Months After Displacement

In July 2021, SDCI Buildings Manager Stephen Hinch issued a Notice of Violation documenting 148 violations at 8201 Aurora Ave N covering all floors, the exterior, and the boiler room. This NOV was issued approximately 11 months after the August 2020 complaints. The public record does not document what triggered the 2021 NOV or what relationship it bears, if any, to the August 2020 complaint closures. The 148-violation count in July 2021 at a property from which tenants had been displaced in August 2020 raises questions the available record does not answer.

6

Construction Permits Issued to Same Owner Following Displacement

Following the August 2020 displacement events and the July 2021 NOV, the City of Seattle issued construction permits to Myung Ha Kang (Ryan Kang) for work at 8201 Aurora Ave N associated with a motel-to-hotel conversion. The Department of Housing conditioned the issuance of a transient accommodations license on permit compliance. The public record does not document that the permit issuance process was conditioned on resolution of the August 2020 complaint closures or the July 2021 NOV. The same entity that was the subject of 13 complaints for mass eviction during a COVID-19 moratorium subsequently received City construction permits for the same property.

Section 06 — Systemic Context

How This Property Fits the Citywide Pattern

The Everspring Inn is an extreme example of a pattern documented across STLCA's full citywide analysis: complaints closed as "Admin Closure / Voluntary Compliance" without documented inspections. The citywide uninspected closure rate for landlord-tenant complaints is 59.8%. At 8201 Aurora Ave N in August 2020, the uninspected closure rate was 100% — 13 of 13 complaints.

The "Voluntary Compliance" designation, applied across 12 of 13 closures at Everspring, is the same designation that appears in STLCA's citywide analysis as the most common closure type for cases with no inspection on record. The designation does not require a documented inspection — and in cases at Everspring, it was applied to situations where tenants described being physically locked out of their units.

District 6, where 8201 Aurora Ave N is located, has an uninspected closure rate of 62.0% across 6,634 landlord-tenant complaints. The Everspring August 2020 event exceeds the district baseline by 38 percentage points.

The simultaneity of the closures — 12 cases closed on a single date by a single inspector — is also notable in the context of STLCA's analysis. Bulk same-day closures on large complaint sets are not routine in the broader dataset and raise methodological questions about how each case was individually reviewed before closure.

The COVID-19 moratorium period (2020) corresponds to the highest uninspected closure rate year in STLCA's citywide eviction complaint analysis: 95.3% of eviction complaints closed without a documented inspection in 2020.

What This Record Raises

The August 2020 complaint record at 8201 Aurora Ave N documents a concentrated enforcement event during a period of heightened legal protection for residential tenants. The available public record does not explain how 13 complaints alleging mass displacement during an active state eviction moratorium were each resolved as "Admin Closure" or "Voluntary Compliance" within 14 days and without a documented field inspection.

The record also does not explain why the property subsequently received construction permits from the City — or whether those permit decisions were made with awareness of the August 2020 complaint history. The July 2021 NOV's 148 violations suggest that significant physical deficiencies existed at the property. The relationship between those conditions and the August 2020 displacement events is not addressed in the public record.

STLCA presents this case study as part of its independent research series on Seattle's housing enforcement record. The facts documented here are drawn entirely from public records. The questions the record raises are appropriate for the City Council and City Auditor to assess as part of any review of SDCI's complaint closure practices during the COVID-19 emergency period. Full complaint extracts, methodology, and data documentation are available from STLCA upon request.
