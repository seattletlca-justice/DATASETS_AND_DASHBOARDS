Quintessa Apartments is a 12-story residential building in Seattle's Pioneer Square/First Hill area. SDCI records show 100 code complaint and enforcement records filed between June 2011 and December 2025 — spanning elevator outages, fire safety door failures, no-heat and no-hot-water emergencies, roach and bedbug infestations, flooding, and repeated eviction pressure on tenants.

Of the 87 records classified as landlord-tenant cases under STLCA's LLT filter (RecordTypeDesc: blank, Emergency, or LandLord/Tenant), 72% were closed with no inspection date recorded — consistent with the citywide pattern (62.3%) but exceeding it by nearly 10 percentage points. Only 6 Notices of Violation were issued across the entire 14-year period.

Complaints document the same mechanical failures returning year after year without permanent resolution: elevator breakdowns first recorded in 2011 persisting through 2025, a boiler that fails repeatedly leaving tenants without heat and hot water, and emergency exit stairwells cited for hazardous conditions across multiple years.

Finding 01 — Infrastructure Failure

Fourteen Years of Elevator Failures, Never Resolved

The building's two elevators have been the subject of 16 SDCI complaints since 2011. The pattern is consistent: one or both elevators fail, tenants file complaints, management promises action, SDCI responds with housing notes or closes without inspection, and the elevators fail again. No permanent mechanical resolution is documented in the public record.

The severity escalated in 2021 when a basement flood knocked out one elevator for nearly four months, leaving a 12-story building's residents — including elderly and disabled tenants — dependent on a single elevator. By July 2024, both elevators were simultaneously out of service. In June 2025, the 9th-floor elevator button went offline, and tenants expressed concern that emergency personnel would be unable to reach upper floors.

Finding 02 — Life Safety

Fire Exits Compromised — Multiple Years, No Permanent Fix

Emergency exit stairwells at 201 Yesler Way were the subject of repeated complaints between 2018 and 2021. Issues documented include an exit door fused shut due to urine decay, a fire door that would not close and was out of SFD compliance, an emergency stairwell ending in a locked hallway with no way out, and stairwells containing large dog droppings described as a "severe health and safety issue."

Finding 03 — Habitability

No Heat, No Hot Water, Infestations — Recurring Across a Decade

Hot water and heat failures at 201 Yesler Way are documented from 2011 through 2025. The boiler that supplies building heat and hot water has failed repeatedly, with multi-day outages described across at least six separate years. In June 2025, tenants reported four consecutive days without hot water — the boiler failed again in September 2025.

Pest infestations — roaches and bedbugs — were documented from 2021 through 2022, with one complaint describing a tenant displaced from their unit for 14 of the prior 20 months due to "repeated bedbug infestations." A separate 2021 complaint described "severe roach infestation attracting rodents" throughout the building.

Finding 04 — Tenant Rights

Eviction Pressure, Retaliation, and Privacy Violations

Tenant rights complaints at 201 Yesler Way span 2018–2025 and include illegal eviction notices, lockouts, unauthorized unit entry, retaliation against tenants who filed court petitions, and — in 2025 — disclosure of tenant Social Security numbers to a third-party money lender without tenant consent.

01

Eviction Pressure — 14-Day Notices, Court Actions

Records filed in 2021 (005862-21CP, 007228-21CP), 2022 (002357-22CP, 002875-22CP, 011719-22CP), and 2024 (004040-24CP) document tenants receiving 14-day pay-or-vacate notices and court eviction proceedings. Several complaints reference eviction notices combined with habitability violations — broken appliances, infestation, flooding — filed simultaneously in the same complaint. All resolved as "POTA" (phone or technical assistance) referrals with no inspections conducted.

Finding 05 — Enforcement Analysis

The Closure Pattern: "No Contact Information" as Administrative Shield

A recurring closure reason across 201 Yesler Way complaints is "No contact information provided — Admin Closure." SDCI applied this reason to fire safety, elevator, and habitability complaints — effectively converting legitimate life-safety concerns into administrative non-events without inspection or follow-up.

This pattern is consistent with STLCA's citywide finding that 62.3% of all LLT cases lack an inspection date, and that the "Admin Closure" mechanism is frequently used to resolve complaints without documented field response. At 201 Yesler Way, the rate exceeds the citywide average by nearly 10 points.

Documented Closure Language — 201 Yesler Way

"Not an SDCI issue. No contact information provided." (Records 002628-21CP, 002631-21CP, 006654-21CP, 008728-21CP)

"Withdrawn — No contact information provided to offer referral." (Records 000104-24CP, 011857-24CP, 011858-24CP)

"Calling and left 2 voicemails for tenant." (Record 008207-24CP — both elevators and boiler failing)

"Not a SDCI issue. Forwarded anonymous complaint." (Elevator stuck between floors, 2021)

The building's RRIO registration history documents at least four distinct RRIO registration numbers appearing in resolution notes across different years (001-0002688, 001-0004720-001R, 001-0100953, 001-0150990). This suggests ownership or management transitions that may have fragmented the enforcement record across multiple registration cycles, with each transition potentially resetting the documented complaint history from SDCI's perspective.

What the Record Shows

201 Yesler Way (Quintessa Apartments) is a 12-story residential building in District 1 that SDCI data shows receiving 100 complaint records over 14 years — 87 classified as landlord-tenant cases, 72% of which were closed without a documented inspection. Sixteen separate elevator complaints span the entire 14-year period with no documented permanent mechanical resolution. Fire exit safety concerns were raised across 2018, 2021, and remain unresolved in the public record.

The data pattern at this property reflects the same enforcement gap documented citywide: complaints are received and logged; inspections are frequently not conducted; and the same conditions recur year after year. As of the December 2025 data pull, five complaints at this address remain under investigation, including a boiler failure, a privacy violation, and a complaint alleging a tenant was displaced by unresolved maintenance conditions and then charged $8,000.
