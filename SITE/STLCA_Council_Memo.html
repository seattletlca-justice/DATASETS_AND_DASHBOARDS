To: Seattle City Council From: Seattle Tenant Landlord Compliance Authority (STLCA) Date: March 2026 Re: Housing Enforcement Documentation Analysis, 2003–2025 — Findings, Evidence, and Recommended Actions

Purpose

This memorandum presents the core findings from STLCA’s independent analysis of Seattle Department of Construction & Inspections (SDCI) housing enforcement records. The analysis covers 233,543 complaint and enforcement records from 2003 through 2025, sourced from the Seattle Open Data Portal.

The purpose is narrow and measurable: to document what the public record shows about complaint handling, inspection documentation, case resolution, and compliance with a legally required annual reporting obligation. All figures cited here are drawn directly from the City’s own published dataset and are independently verifiable.

Core Findings

1. Most landlord-tenant cases close without a documented inspection result.

59.8% of closed or completed landlord-tenant cases in the dataset contain no recorded inspection result (LastInspResult field is blank). This is the central accountability gap presented in this briefing. The rate varies by district: District 7 (Downtown) = 67.2%; District 3 (Capitol Hill) = 62.9%; District 1 (West Seattle) = 56.6%. No district falls below 52%.

2. Inspection dates are missing in the majority of landlord-tenant cases.

62.3% of landlord-tenant cases in the dataset contain no inspection date (LastInspDate field is blank). A missing field does not prove no inspection occurred. It does establish that the public enforcement record does not document that event in the field designated to capture it.

3. Thousands of cases remain unresolved, some for many years.

5,936 landlord-tenant cases remain open or unresolved in the current dataset. Data quality note: The oldest record in the dataset (November 2003) is a relocation payment to the Seattle Public Library—proof that the raw data itself is corrupted and requires independent audit to verify what is and isn't a real enforcement case. The median age of currently open cases is 852 days.

4. A legally required annual reporting obligation has not been fulfilled.

Ordinance 125054, passed unanimously 8-0 by City Council in June 2016, required SDCI to report annually to Council beginning June 15, 2017. The required elements include inquiry counts, inspection results, outcomes, and eviction cross-references. Nine reporting cycles have passed. No reports produced under this obligation appear in the public record reviewed for this briefing. When directly asked, SDCI’s RRIO Manager stated in writing: “We’ve never prepared such a report… 22.206.180.J doesn’t exist.” The signed ordinance is a public record and is included in the full documentation package.

Illustrative Examples

The percentages above represent individual cases. The following three examples document how the broader pattern appears in specific buildings and obligations.

Fire Safety / Enforcement Delay — District 7
The Addison on 4th — 308 4th Ave S

A Seattle Fire Department safety alert was issued November 6, 2019. SDCI did not conduct an inspection for 29 days (December 5). A Notice of Violation was issued December 13. A fire occurred January 7, 2020 — 62 days after the initial alert. The public enforcement record shows no financial penalties. Post-fire complaints filed against the same property subsequently disappeared from the public database.

Illegal Evictions During COVID Moratorium — District 6
Everspring Inn — 8201 Aurora Ave N

On August 18, 2020, 13 complaints were filed in a single day documenting mass displacement of elderly, disabled, and low-income tenants during the active COVID-19 eviction moratorium. All 13 cases were administratively closed “Voluntary Compliance” within days. The public record documents no inspections associated with any of those closures. Tenants were displaced to the street within 24 hours.

Reporting Obligation Denied in Writing — Citywide
Ordinance 125054 — Nine Missing Annual Reports

When STLCA inquired about the annual reports required by Ord. 125054, SDCI’s RRIO Manager responded: “We’ve never prepared such a report… 22.206.180.J doesn’t exist… makes a jump from I to K.” The signed ordinance includes Section J and is publicly available. Council was entitled to receive this report every year beginning in 2017.

Recommended Actions

This memorandum does not ask Council to adopt a conclusion unsupported by the data. It asks Council to act on measurable deficiencies in documentation, oversight, and statutory compliance.

  1. Direct a formal performance audit of SDCI housing enforcement practice. Review case closure methodology, inspection documentation standards, handling of cases that remain open for multi-year periods, and consistency between reported outcomes and operational claims.
  2. Require completion of overdue annual accountability reports. Direct SDCI to produce the annual reports required by Ordinance 125054 for each year since 2017, and establish an enforcement mechanism to ensure ongoing compliance.
  3. Establish an independent oversight and review mechanism. Create a formal process for reviewing housing enforcement outcomes independent of the department’s own internal reporting. This is the reporting framework Ord. 125054 was meant to produce.
  4. Require cross-referencing of eviction activity against active habitability complaints. A tenant should not receive an eviction notice while unresolved habitability complaints remain active against the same property. The current dataset does not document this cross-check occurring.

Methodological Note

Source dataset: City of Seattle Open Data Portal, Code Complaints and Violations dataset, extracted December 31, 2025. Total records: 233,543.

Landlord-tenant classification: Cases classified as landlord-tenant related where RecordTypeDesc was null/blank, contained “Emergency,” or contained “LandLord/Tenant.” Total LLT records: 58,761.

“Closed with no inspection result”: Cases where StatusCurrent = Closed or Completed and LastInspResult is blank in the public extract.

District counts: 2,671 records (1.1%) could not be geocoded to a district in the SDCI source data. These are included in all citywide totals but excluded from individual district counts.

Full methodology, field definitions, source data, and analysis scripts are available in the STLCA public repository.
