What STLCA Does

STLCA does not investigate individual cases or represent tenants or landlords. We apply structured, rulebook-driven analysis to public data — and report what the record shows and what it cannot show.

📊

Audit-Grade Analysis

Every metric is defined before it is measured. We publish our methodology, field mappings, and limitations alongside our findings. No number appears without a source and a definition.

🌍

Public Data Only

All analysis draws exclusively from the City of Seattle Open Data Portal. Our findings can be independently replicated by anyone with access to the same public records.

⚖️

No Conclusions Beyond the Data

A missing inspection date does not prove an inspection did not happen. It proves the public record does not document one. We are precise about what the data can and cannot establish.

How We Measure — Methodology Summary

Data source: City of Seattle Open Data Portal — "Code Complaints and Violations" dataset, extracted December 31, 2025. 233,543 records spanning 2003–2025.

LLT classification: Records are classified as landlord-tenant (LLT) where RecordTypeDesc is null/blank, or contains "Emergency," or contains "LandLord/Tenant." This captures 58,761 records citywide. Note: 2,671 records (1.1%) could not be assigned to a council district in the source data and are excluded from district-level counts.

What "no inspection date" means: The field LastInspDate is blank in the public record. STLCA does not assert that no inspection occurred — only that the public record does not document one.

What "closed with no inspection result" means: StatusCurrent is Closed or Completed AND LastInspResult is blank. This is the central STLCA accountability metric: cases the system treats as finished while the public extract contains no structured outcome record.

A Structural Gap in Accountability

Seattle's Office of Inspector General provides independent oversight of the Seattle Police Department. No equivalent body exists for the Seattle Department of Construction and Inspections. The question of whether an enforcement agency's own records can serve as the sole measure of its performance is a standard concern in public administration — one that peer cities have addressed through independent review mechanisms.

1
What an independent performance audit would examine A structured review of SDCI's code enforcement practices, data recording standards, and case closure procedures — assessing whether the public record accurately reflects enforcement activity, and whether closure rates correspond to resolved conditions or administrative disposition.
2
What a permanent oversight structure would provide A formal, independent review mechanism — comparable in function to the OIG model — would allow constituents to seek review of enforcement decisions and give policymakers a verified basis for assessing agency performance beyond the agency's own reporting.
