Seattle City Council Rollout Letter — STLCA Data Findings
Councilmembers,
Our independent review of the Seattle Department of Construction & Inspections (SDCI) complaint and inspection records uncovered systemic failures in the City’s own data. These failures pose serious questions about enforcement reliability, data integrity, and our ability to protect tenants. With this letter, the Seattle Tenant‑Landlord Compliance Authority (STLCA) aims to give the full Council the evidence needed to act.
Discovery Timeline
July 6–20, 2025: STLCA noticed that nearly every complaint record in SDCI’s public portal lacked a last‑inspection date and a result. We emailed data publisher Moon Callison seeking answers; no response was received. Within days, SDCI quietly began populating dates, but coverage never surpassed ~20 %.
This coincidence suggests that STLCA’s inquiry triggered data manipulation rather than a genuine, transparent fix.
Core Findings – Data Failures
Missing and corrupted fields: Thousands of complaint records lack usable timestamps for the complaint’s last inspection; dates were either missing entirely or corrupted. Without a reliable date, there is no audit trail for when SDCI inspected a property.
Blank inspection results: The majority of closed cases have no outcome recorded. “Completed” often means “no documented inspection,” and “Closed” often means “case abandoned.”
Undefined complaint types: A key field (RecordTypeDesc) is blank on most records. Without this, the City cannot distinguish between habitability complaints and administrative issues.
Manual data cleanup required: STLCA had to hand‑clean and normalize more than 200,000 fields just to make sense of the records. No auditor has ever delivered this level of analysis to the Council.
Quantitative Findings – The Numbers
Using SDCI’s October 30 and 31 datasets (Phase 1 output files), STLCA calculated the following cross‑district metrics:
Metric
Evidence
Records missing or corrupted inspection dates
~73 % of inspection records lacked a usable timestamp for analysis.
Blank inspection result fields
89 % of cases marked “Closed” contained no inspection result.
Functional audit trail
0 % – there is no complete audit trail to verify enforcement claims or track resolution timelines.
Complaints flagged for potential illegal or no‑cause eviction
In total, 2 021 eviction/retaliation complaints across all seven districts showed text indicating non‑compliance with the Just Cause Eviction Ordinance.
Complaints flagged during the eviction moratorium
524 cases occurred while the City’s moratorium was in place. District 7 alone had 88 such complaints.
Blank “Last Inspection Date” across all fields
23.27 % (214 444 fields) of four critical enforcement data points are missing.
Tenant complaints without any inspection result
61.23 % of tenant habitability complaints have no recorded inspection outcome.
Punchline: “Completed” cases are often documented without an inspection, and “Closed” cases often show no outcome at all.
Pattern Analysis and Moratorium Enforcement
STLCA’s text analysis flagged patterns showing:
Clusters of recurring complaints against the same properties (e.g., dozens of habitability complaints for the same building over several years) with no enforcement follow‑up.
Retroactively edited or duplicate entries, making it impossible to track what happened.
Hundreds of cases where properties continued renting during the moratorium, even though their own complaints indicated non‑compliance.
Many properties lacked any valid RRIO registration. While STLCA initially cross‑checked addresses against the RRIO database, we later found that some “unregistered” statuses were due to lapsed but reinstated certificates. Until SDCI provides an up‑to‑date RRIO registry, we exclude those numbers to avoid mischaracterizing landlords.
Historical Accountability
The failures detailed above predate the current Council. When SDCI absorbed tenant‑landlord enforcement from the Human Services Department in the early 2010s, power consolidated without transparent oversight. Since then, the City’s tenant‑protection infrastructure has weakened. Current Councilmembers inherited this dysfunctional system; STLCA’s role is to help you fix it.
Councilmember Kettle & Solomon — Your Warnings Validated
Councilmember Kettle (District 7) correctly warned that SDCI’s dual role as building‑permits agency and tenant‑landlord enforcer violates good governance. Our evidence shows that permitting‑related complaints (noise, construction, zoning) are prioritized, while habitability complaints languish with no inspection or result.
Councilmember Solomon (District 2) observed that SDCI has rarely complied with reporting requirements. STLCA’s analysis confirms this: mandatory annual reports were never delivered until 2024, and even then, the data was incomplete.
We highlight these points not to assign blame, but to recognize your foresight and to encourage your colleagues to join the push for independent oversight.
Why District 7 Is First
Out of all districts, District 7 (Councilmember Kettle’s district) recorded:
78.56 % of habitability complaints with no inspection result — the highest rate.
290 eviction complaints flagged for potential no‑cause violations and 88 flagged during the moratorium — again the highest number across districts.
Serial off‑the‑books rental activity at properties like 1110 8th Ave (48 complaints with no RRIO registration), 823 Madison St (39 complaints), and 700 5th Ave (36 complaints), showing systemic neglect.
Given the severity of these numbers, STLCA prepared the District 7 dossier first. Dossiers for all other districts are ready and will be delivered once Councilmembers review the District 7 findings.
What STLCA Is Asking
Freeze SDCI’s control over tenant‑landlord oversight. Shift habitability and eviction enforcement to an independent body.
Launch a forensic audit of SDCI’s complaint records, including a full timeline of retroactive edits and missing data.
Require SDCI to produce a verified RRIO registry so the City can accurately distinguish unregistered rentals from lapsed certificates.
Engage STLCA as your data partner. We have already normalized and reconciled the City’s data; we can help you monitor compliance going forward.
Conclusion
Seattle’s enforcement failures are not a matter of politics — they are a matter of arithmetic. When 73 % of inspection dates are missing, 89 % of cases lack outcomes, and 214 444 critical data fields are blank, it is impossible to say enforcement is working. STLCA exists to make “Completed” and “Closed” mean something again.
We hope you will review the District 7 dossier and work with STLCA to rebuild tenant‑landlord oversight. Please visit STLCA’s secure portal to request your district’s dossier or contact our office for early access.
Respectfully,
Kimberly Coleman
Seattle Tenant‑Landlord Compliance Authority (STLCA)

Seattle’s Rental Oversight Data: Findings and Urgent Next Steps
Seattle City Councilmembers:

The Seattle Tenant‑Landlord Compliance Authority (STLCA) conducted an independent audit of the Seattle Department of Construction & Inspections (SDCI) complaint and inspection records. This audit uncovered systemic failures that jeopardise tenant safety and place the City at risk of liability. Our core findings are detailed below. We urge Council to take immediate corrective action and reconsider SDCI’s role in tenant‑landlord enforcement.

1. Discovery Timeline
Early July 2025: STLCA discovered that the Open Data Portal lacked Last Inspection Date entries for most complaints.
July 6–20, 2025: After STLCA alerted the City’s data publisher (Moon Callison) to this issue, inspection dates suddenly began appearing in the dataset. However, even after this update, less than 20 % of complaints show any inspection data.
As of October 30, 2025: Data still showed massive gaps. This sequence indicates the dataset was manipulated without explanation and still fails to capture basic enforcement outcomes.
2. Core Data Failures
Our audit of 228,259 complaints identified:

214,444 missing data points across four key enforcement fields (Last Inspection Date, Last Inspection Result, Status, and Record Type) – roughly 23.27 % of all entries.
61.23 % of tenant‑habitability complaints have no inspection result at all.
89.24 % of cases marked “Closed” have no recorded inspection result, suggesting these were abandoned rather than resolved.
8,021‑day “active” investigations with no outcome – some cases have remained open for 22 years with no inspection.

Key takeaways:

Thousands of complaints per district remain unregistered or unmatched to RRIO properties, undermining enforcement.
Hundreds of units were rented without RRIO certificates; address verification showed that even after matching longitude/latitude, most remained unregistered.
Nearly half of eviction complaints flagged as potential illegal or no‑cause took place during the COVID‑19 moratorium, violating Seattle’s just‑cause ordinance.
4. Systemic Pattern
Across all districts, we observed:

Duplicate or retroactively edited entries – addresses repeated dozens of times with identical complaints but no additional action.
Habitability complaints tied to construction permits often received follow‑up, while stand‑alone tenant health complaints languished.
Vacant buildings and weed abatement cases often resulted in fees, but no evidence shows SDCI collected hundreds of thousands of dollars owed.
5. Structural Accountability Context
These failures pre‑date the current Council. They trace back to the 2010s, when tenant‑landlord enforcement was moved from Human Services into SDCI. SDCI’s dual role—issuing permits and policing habitability—created a conflict of interest that allowed data integrity to erode and vulnerable tenants to be neglected. Consolidated power and a lack of transparency led to widespread non‑compliance.

6. Validation from Councilmembers Kettle and Solomon
Councilmember Bob Kettle has been correct in his insistence that SDCI should not oversee tenant‑landlord enforcement. Our data confirm that this department has mismanaged complaints, suppressed or edited crucial data, and left tenants in unsafe conditions. Councilmember Andrew J. Lewis originally authored the ordinance to create STLCA after the Office of Housing’s failures; SDCI’s mismanagement shows the wisdom of that decision. Councilmember Tammy J. Morales previously noted SDCI’s non‑compliance—our findings validate that claim. Councilmember Dan Strauss warned about missing inspection records; this audit quantifies that negligence.

7. Rollout and Next Steps
We will release the District 7 dossier first, in deference to Councilmember Kettle’s district and his leadership on this issue. Dossiers for Districts 1–6 will follow, each password‑protected and accessible through the STLCA portal. Councilmembers may request early access to datasets, notice‑of‑violation timelines, and property‑level case studies via the STLCA website’s contact form.

8. Conclusion
Seattle’s enforcement failures are not political — they’re mathematical. The data show a pattern of neglect and concealment that endangers tenants and exposes the City to liability. STLCA exists to make “Completed” and “Closed” mean something again. We urge the Council to:

Reassign tenant‑landlord enforcement to an independent authority with data integrity safeguards.
Hold a public hearing on the July 6–20 dataset manipulation and the missing inspection records.
Investigate unregistered properties and enforce RRIO requirements.
Audit SDCI’s fee collection and duplication practices to recover lost revenue.
Respectfully,

Kimberly Coleman
Seattle Tenant‑Landlord Compliance Authority (STLCA)

import pandas as pd

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt


trend = pd.read_csv("Missingness_Trend_By_Year.csv")


# drop the "Average" row for plotting

plot_df = trend[trend["Year"] != "Average"].copy()

plot_df["Year"] = plot_df["Year"].astype(int)


plt.figure()

plt.plot(plot_df["Year"], plot_df["LastInspDate_PctMissing"], label="Missing LastInspDate (%)")

plt.plot(plot_df["Year"], plot_df["LastInspResult_PctMissing"], label="Missing LastInspResult (%)")

plt.plot(plot_df["Year"], plot_df["RecordTypeDesc_PctMissing"], label="Missing RecordTypeDesc (%)")


plt.axvline(2018, linestyle="--")

plt.text(2018 + 0.2,

         max(plot_df["LastInspDate_PctMissing"].max(),

             plot_df["LastInspResult_PctMissing"].max()) * 0.8,

         "Record integrity collapse begins\n(2018)",

         rotation=0,

         fontsize=9)


plt.title("Seattle Code Enforcement Data Integrity Collapse by Year")

plt.xlabel("Year complaint was opened")

plt.ylabel("% of records with missing required data")

plt.legend()

plt.tight_layout()

plt.savefig("Data_Integrity_Collapse_Timeline.png", dpi=300)


print("Created Data_Integrity_Collapse_Timeline.png")
